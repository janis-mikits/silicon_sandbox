# World representation and chunking research

**Date:** 29 September 2026  
**Status:** Research for [original backlog R-04](docs/original-research-backlog.md#world-representation-and-chunking). The user accepted the sparse authored grid, generated floor representation, bounded render regions with vertical subdivisions, and offscreen simulation independence on 29 September 2026. One versus multiple floor objects remains a performance-driven implementation choice. The canonical decision is in [project vision and world](docs/project-vision-and-world.md#world-data-and-rendering-architecture). Remaining measured texture, mesh, and distant-LOD choices are in the [current research backlog](docs/research-backlog.md); the Minecraft-mod survey is complete.

## Recommendation

Use **three separate representations**:

1. **Authored world data:** A sparse, grid-indexed record of player-placed blocks, components, modules, and connectors, plus configured world generation. The unbreakable default floor can be represented by a generator/rule instead of one million individually stored block objects for a provisional 1,000 × 1,000 floor. Its visible surface and collision can be produced efficiently while preserving the specified one-block-thick sandstone behavior, grid targeting, outline, and configurable terrain settings. This is a proposed representation, not a change to the floor rules. [World](docs/project-vision-and-world.md#default-sandbox-and-boundaries) · [Building](docs/building-and-interface.md#player-presentation-and-tagging)
2. **View-dependent render data:** Partition placed geometry into bounded spatial regions with vertical subdivisions. Build or batch visible geometry for those regions and update only regions affected by an edit (plus neighbors where a shared boundary changes). Hide or unload *render representations* outside view/distance as appropriate. Do **not** require every region containing a circuit to remain rendered for that circuit to run. A region may need more than one mesh/renderer for materials, connector shapes, and changing signal visuals; “one chunk = one GameObject/draw call” is not an invariant. [Unity frustum/occlusion culling](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/OcclusionCulling.html) · [Draw-call methods](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/optimizing-draw-calls-choose-method.html)
3. **Whole-circuit simulation graph:** Derive this from the authored design and run it independently of view or render regions. Offscreen electrical events remain active, and a visible region reads the latest settled values when drawn again. This is already required by the [accepted simulator architecture](docs/digital-simulation.md#runtime-architecture-decision) and [clock rules](docs/circuit-time-and-clock.md#clock-and-event-model).

Spatial regions are an indexing and rendering tool, **not an electrical boundary**. Wires and Net Links may connect across regions without changing their net semantics. A later graphics streaming policy must likewise not delete active circuit state.

## Answers to the backlog's specific questions

| Question | Research answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Should chunks be 16 × 16 × full world height?** | Do not lock that shape now. A full-height region up to the provisional 300-block limit makes vertical visibility and local edits coarse when builds occupy different heights. Shorter vertical sections, or another bounded spatial grouping, can localize mesh work and culling. Exact 2D/3D dimensions should be configurable implementation parameters and measured in the actual first playable, not treated as a Minecraft rule. This is an engineering inference from the editable 3D world and rendering requirements. |
| **Must populated chunks stay rendered so their circuits run?** | **No.** The canonical spec explicitly requires offscreen simulation. Keep authored data and the simulation graph active; only rendered geometry is view-dependent. [Circuit time](docs/circuit-time-and-clock.md#clock-and-event-model) |
| **Should each chunk be one GameObject?** | Not as a requirement. Fewer draw submissions can help, but a huge combined mesh has coarse culling and expensive local rebuilds; materials and dynamic signal visuals may require separate batches. Unity offers multiple draw-call methods with different costs and compatibility. Profile the mix actually used. [Unity draw-call guide](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/optimizing-draw-calls-choose-method.html) · [GPU instancing limits](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/GPUInstancing.html) |
| **Can the unbreakable sandstone ground be one entity/mesh?** | **Yes as a rendering/storage strategy.** The gameplay contract still says one-block thickness, fixed unbreakability, configurable material/dimensions, grid-based placement, and a thin outline for the aimed floor cell. Those can be computed from world coordinates and shown with an outline overlay; individual GameObjects for each floor tile are unnecessary. A single giant render mesh is not mandatory if tiling improves culling or texture handling. This is a design inference, not a Unity requirement. [World](docs/project-vision-and-world.md#default-sandbox-and-boundaries) · [Building](docs/building-and-interface.md#player-presentation-and-tagging) |
| **Would chunks make circuit simulation difficult or slow?** | Only if circuit ownership is tied to rendered chunks. With a whole-circuit graph and explicit cross-region links, simulation does not care whether its visual pieces are in one or many regions. Edits crossing region boundaries still need correct graph updates. [Simulator architecture](docs/digital-simulation.md#runtime-architecture-decision) |

## Rendering and interaction implications

- **Sparse content:** The full provisional bounding volume is 1,000 × 1,000 × 300 = 300 million grid cells. Allocating one managed object or Unity GameObject for every cell would create work for empty space. A sparse index holds actual placed content; the generated floor and boundary rules are separate. This arithmetic supports the recommendation but is not a measured memory benchmark.
- **One-object floor clarification:** A single floor GameObject can carry a simple broad mesh and a box-shaped collider; its physical size need not imply one vertex, collider, or stored record per grid cell. A raycast hit supplies a world-space point from which the aimed grid coordinate can be computed, then a separate outline can mark that cell. Unity culls a renderer using its overall bounds, so a giant floor is coarse-grained for culling; a simple flat mesh can still be inexpensive to keep loaded. One versus a few floor meshes should be judged from texture, lighting, camera, and profiling needs rather than equating world dimensions with mesh complexity. [Unity RaycastHit](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/RaycastHit.html) · [BoxCollider](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/BoxCollider.html) · [Renderer bounds](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/Renderer-bounds.html)
- **Visible faces and repeated meshes:** Excluding covered faces from opaque block geometry may reduce submitted triangles. Repeated gates or blocks may instead benefit from instancing or another batching method. Unity cautions that GPU instancing's overhead can outweigh benefits depending on platform and renderer; do not declare one method universally fastest. [Unity GPU instancing](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/GPUInstancing.html)
- **Editable regions:** Runtime player edits make pre-baked static batching and baked occlusion poor universal answers. Unity states that built-in baked occlusion is unsuitable when scene geometry is generated at runtime, and static batches do not allow individual member transforms to change. Its `MeshData` API can create meshes through the C# Job System if mesh rebuild cost warrants it. [Unity occlusion](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/OcclusionCulling.html) · [Static batching](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/static-batching-enable.html) · [MeshData](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/Mesh.MeshData.html)
- **Precise targeting:** A merged visual mesh must not erase the ability to select one of four channels, a thin wire segment, a specific pin, a junction versus crossing, or a floor cell. Use the authored spatial index to find candidate elements and apply their exact hit rules; visible selection overlays can be separate from batched geometry. This is an implementation inference from [physical targeting](docs/physical-connections.md#pins-and-targeting) and [crowded selection](docs/physical-connections.md#crowded-selection-and-invalid-actions).
- **Changing signal colors:** Electrical state changes should not automatically rebuild all region geometry or wake unrelated Unity objects. Prefer a narrowly updated visual state path and measure its cost. The exact shader, material, or instance-data approach is not chosen here. [Signal visuals](docs/digital-simulation.md#visuals) · [Inspect](docs/inspection-and-diagnostics.md)
- **Frame versus simulation measurement:** Track rendered FPS, achieved simulation speed, visible object/connector counts, and editing latency separately, per the canonical [benchmark](docs/performance-and-platforms.md#benchmark). The user's large-circuit smoothness priority also needs later quantitative workloads and thresholds; the 1,000-gate first-playable benchmark alone does not establish full-game scalability.

## What remains open

1. Region dimensions and vertical section height; scene/render pipeline and material strategy; which categories use generated meshes, GPU instancing, or ordinary renderers. Choose after profiling actual first-playable scenes rather than a disposable comparison prototype, consistent with the user's engine decision.
2. Exact spatial index and update algorithm for connector geometry, collision, selection, and graph changes. It must preserve the [four-channel and explicit-junction rules](docs/physical-connections.md).
3. Terrain texturing, texture atlases/mipmaps, greedy meshing, LOD, and more advanced culling; route remaining work through the [current research backlog](docs/research-backlog.md) as needed. Minecraft techniques are candidate ideas, not requirements to copy.
4. Far-distance rendering and world-data streaming policy for much larger builds. Any policy must preserve simulation state and saved design; do not let an offscreen visual optimization deactivate circuits.

**Decision status:** The user confirmed sparse authored world storage, generated floor representation, and bounded view-dependent render regions with vertical subdivisions. Rendering visibility never controls whether circuits simulate. The floor must satisfy its specified behavior, while one versus multiple render objects is left to measured performance and implementation simplicity. Exact chunk dimensions, GameObject/renderer grouping, render pipeline, and mesh algorithm remain open.
