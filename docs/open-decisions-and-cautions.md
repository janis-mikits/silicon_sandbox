## Design points not fully settled

- Simulation-time precision, checked timestamp encoding/overflow behavior, no-drift world-clock scheduling, the first-playable 0.1–100 Hz range, and supported-subset event ordering are settled in [Section 2](circuit-time-and-clock.md#first-playable-scheduler-contract). The later player-facing frequency range, exact phase of newly created derived clocks, and independent free-running source frequencies/phases remain open until those features.
- Exact control for creating a split point and every split-map validation rule, including overlapping selections and duplicate mapped outputs. Width Adapter handling of unknown sign bits and precise truncation-selection UI also deserve examples.
- Right-clicking a Constant Logic Source body versus targeting its free pin for connector placement, and contextual Shift + right-click force-placement versus future connector concatenation, are settled in [building and interface](building-and-interface.md#default-controls). The actual multi-bit concatenation feature remains later work.
- View/world-axis meaning of a circuit’s “bottom-right corner” in three dimensions and the menu or library action from which Expand Module is invoked. The confirmed result is a new editable design copy with no live-state transfer.
- Precise five-minute undo timestamp behavior after reopening a world, especially if the world was closed longer than five minutes. The accepted promise is that the saved five-minute history remains available after reopening.
- Detailed inventory search, favorites, categories, and module-library organization. The Minecraft-style inventory/hotbar reference alone does not specify these features.
- The [first-playable benchmark protocol](performance-and-platforms.md#benchmark) fixes the Standard preset conditions, 1,000-AND mixed scene, 10 Hz active case, reporting, camera-case durations, and stutter investigation criterion. Choose and freeze the actual Windows/Mac machines and scene artifact before acceptance measurement, then report measured 100 ms/2 s/5 s response results. Later large-operation budgets and large-circuit workloads remain open until those features.
- Full educational lesson map and unlock list, formal distinction between built versus supplied larger systems, hint content, and course-specific scope. Education was deliberately lowest priority.
- The [optimization-fidelity rule](digital-simulation.md#optimization-fidelity) now fixes observable authored layout, signals, diagnostics, event behavior, instance state, and gate counts for the supported subset. Later component/feature additions extend that invariant set before they are optimized.
- The [first-playable configuration compatibility rule](project-vision-and-world.md#definitions-and-extensibility) fixes which initial settings are player-facing and snapshots resolved generation/component values in version 1 saves. The complete later-game developer-versus-player policy remains open for later features.
- Detailed menu styling, audio beyond the invalid-action sound, accessibility options, Windows/macOS packaging and distribution details. These were flagged as later design-document topics, not resolved in the 12 sections.
- Edge behavior of mixed-value harness color in ordinary world view, beyond the confirmed packed-vector inspection and four-state bit representation.
## Technical interpretation cautions

- An undriven ordinary net as Z and a four-state variable as X are useful distinctions. Avoid claiming that every disconnected SystemVerilog port kind universally resolves to Z without naming the type and connection context.
- The game’s equal-strength driver simplification, strict physical width refusal, MUX unknown-select merge, SR invalid-state choice, counter overflow pulse, pulse queue, and missing-module placeholder are explicit product rules. Compare them to IEEE/VCS with focused tests where equivalence matters; do not present each as an IEEE-mandated default.
- The title “latest SystemVerilog” is a maintenance instruction, not an automatic promise of every future standard or every language construct. Verify the current IEEE revision and relevant VCS options before implementing or claiming conformance. Preserve the exact vendor-protection paragraph in [Section 1](digital-simulation.md).
- The world save is design plus specified persistent data, not a complete runtime checkpoint. Snapshotting after a safe time slot and writing asynchronously are compatible, but implementation must handle a nonsettling slot through the convergence rule.
- Module-version migration starts fresh state. Pin-compatible ports do not imply register, RAM, or NVM data compatibility. Warning and future migration/backup UI require separate implementation detail.
## Additional unresolved choices from the merge

- Architecture. The engine and starting version are Unity 6.3 LTS, Editor 6000.3.24f1; see [project vision](project-vision-and-world.md#definitions-and-extensibility). The primary runtime is a purpose-built C# event-driven four-state simulator separate from Unity visuals; see [digital simulation](digital-simulation.md#runtime-architecture-decision). The [first-playable component scope and internal correctness matrix](delivery-and-acceptance.md#first-playable-internal-correctness-matrix) are settled. Broader SystemVerilog language/conformance claims and their external VCS configuration remain later work; VCS is not a shipped dependency.
- Benchmark procedure. The [first-playable protocol](performance-and-platforms.md#benchmark) is fixed for development and acceptance. Actual Windows/Mac hardware, the saved scene artifact, and measured results must be recorded when available; the ordinary-Steam-PC representativeness claim requires current evidence and is not presumed.
- Module families and expansion. Incompatible in-place migration is prohibited; whether incompatible revisions can share a family for new placement is open. Define default inout layout, zero-port size, and whether re-expansion leaves nested modules packaged or recursively expands them. The old nested-expansion suggestion accompanied a rejected live-instance replacement proposal and was not explicitly restored.
- Component completeness. Preserve the original up/down counter concept without inventing downward wrap or underflow rules. Set a divider maximum and clarify reset priority for N=1 passthrough, reset synchrony, and unknown controls. Define MUX data-width range, decoder/encoder unknown and disabled/no-input behavior, remaining storage-component X/Z-control behavior, and precise memory/register component interfaces and capacities. The shared [positive-edge detection rule](circuit-time-and-clock.md#four-state-positive-edge-detection) and [SR flip-flop S/R=X/Z policy](components-and-rtl-timing.md#sr-storage) are settled; other components' responses to uncertain data and controls remain open. The original MUX input-count range remains 2–32; the 128-bit pin ceiling does not automatically widen every default module.
- Connector editing. The first-playable [one-bit route/join schema](first-playable-data-schema.md#connector-geometry-and-joins), two-piece split identity rule, and ordinary tag-merge rule are settled. Combined/split harness replacement, channel/mapping overlaps and repeated-net fanout, and interactions with general named Net Links remain later feature questions; incompatible edits must still be rejected atomically.
- Presentation and control precedence. First-playable source-body use versus targeted pin, contextual Shift force-placement, identity color versus state color, vertical-axis rotation, and edge/cycle stepping are settled in [building](building-and-interface.md#default-controls) and [connections](physical-connections.md#connector-appearance). Mixed-harness appearance, controller alternate-action mapping, scroll-wheel hotbar versus crowded cycling, and persisted undo timestamp policy remain later feature questions.
- Validation and configuration. The [first-playable internal correctness matrix](delivery-and-acceptance.md#first-playable-internal-correctness-matrix), [safe optimization invariants](digital-simulation.md#optimization-fidelity), and [first-playable configuration rule](project-vision-and-world.md#definitions-and-extensibility) are settled. Broader IEEE/VCS conformance and later feature settings require their own claims/tests; retained research notes are not authoritative answers.
