## How to use this glossary

This file collects **project-specific names and project-specific meanings** used by the canonical design spec. It is a vocabulary guide, not a second source of mechanics or a new design decision. Follow each entry's linked subsystem file for complete behavior, limits, unresolved details, and later changes. General software and digital-electronics terms are omitted unless SiliconSandbox gives them a specific role that an agent might otherwise misread. A term marked *later* is specified or contemplated for a stage after the first playable; its presence here does not move it into that milestone.

## World and authored objects

| Term | Meaning in SiliconSandbox | Full specification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **World** | One local, finite 3D sandbox containing the player's authored build, world settings, and saved player data. Its circuits continue to simulate when out of view. | [World and boundaries](project-vision-and-world.md#default-sandbox-and-boundaries); [saving](saving-and-recovery.md#saved-design-versus-transient-simulation) |
| **Game object** | Umbrella term for a block, component, or module. A connector is a separate connection category. | [Definitions](project-vision-and-world.md#definitions-and-extensibility) |
| **Block / construction block** | A player-placeable building block, such as inventory sandstone or colored scaffolding. It is distinct from a circuit component or packaged module. | [Construction blocks](components-and-rtl-timing.md#construction-blocks); [definitions](project-vision-and-world.md#definitions-and-extensibility) |
| **Scaffolding** | The uniform solid-color construction block offered in the specified sixteen-color palette; it is building material, not an electrical connector. | [Construction blocks](components-and-rtl-timing.md#construction-blocks) |
| **Generated floor** | The default unbreakable, one-block-thick smooth-sandstone floor created from world-generation settings. It is distinct from breakable sandstone placed by a player and need not be stored as individual authored blocks. | [World boundaries](project-vision-and-world.md#default-sandbox-and-boundaries); [world data](project-vision-and-world.md#world-data-and-rendering-architecture) |
| **Bounding wall / world border** | The unbreakable wall surrounding the finite sandbox. Players cannot touch or pass through it; it may be represented by custom non-placeable geometry rather than authored blocks. | [World boundaries](project-vision-and-world.md#default-sandbox-and-boundaries) |
| **Component** | A placed circuit device, such as a gate, storage element, source, or display, with configured behavior and connection pins. | [Definitions](project-vision-and-world.md#definitions-and-extensibility); [component catalog](components-and-rtl-timing.md) |
| **Authored design / blueprint** | The durable description of placed parts, exact 3D layout, connector paths and joins, settings, and referenced module versions. The editable world circuit and each fixed module version have authored designs; the simulator graph and rendered geometry are derived from them. | [Canonical module representation](modules-and-packaging.md#canonical-design-and-runtime-representation); [saved records](first-playable-data-schema.md#shared-authored-design) |
| **Authored grid cell** | A saved integer `[x,y,z]` location: x east, y up, z north. The generated floor is at `y=0`; player placement starts at `y=1`. | [World coordinate convention](project-vision-and-world.md#world-data-and-rendering-architecture); [schema transforms](first-playable-data-schema.md#grid-and-orientation) |
| **Face quadrant / `pointQ`** | One of four connection positions on a cell face in a 2 × 2 arrangement. Version 1 saves the exact point in quarter-cell coordinates as `pointQ`, rather than relying on a camera-relative “top left” label. | [Pins and targeting](physical-connections.md#pins-and-targeting); [grid schema](first-playable-data-schema.md#grid-and-orientation) |
| **Saved orientation** | A placed component's or module's grid-aligned front and top directions. The `forward`/`up` pair represents one of 24 valid 3D orientations; first-playable controls turn only around the vertical axis. | [Post-placement rotation](physical-connections.md#post-placement-rotation); [schema transform](first-playable-data-schema.md#grid-and-orientation) |
| **Render region** | A bounded spatial section of derived visible geometry, with vertical subdivisions, used so local edits update relevant graphics without rebuilding the whole world. It does not bound saved authored data or electrical simulation. Exact region dimensions remain a measured implementation choice. | [World rendering architecture](project-vision-and-world.md#world-data-and-rendering-architecture); [renderer contract](graphics-and-future-tools.md#renderer-boundary-contract) |

## Connections and signals

| Term | Meaning in SiliconSandbox | Full specification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pin** | A component or module input, output, or inout connection carrying 1–128 bits. A connector attaches to a pin through explicit targeting; touching another component does not connect it. | [Definitions](project-vision-and-world.md#definitions-and-extensibility); [pins and targeting](physical-connections.md#pins-and-targeting) |
| **Connector** | Umbrella term for the three physical, visible connection types: **wire**, **harness**, and **Net Link**. It refers to a player-placeable path or stub, not to an entire derived electrical net. | [Terms and capacity](physical-connections.md#terms-and-capacity) |
| **Wire** | A one-bit physical connector. It may route through cells and faces, form explicit junctions, and carry one resolved four-state bit. | [Harness representation](harnesses-and-net-links.md#harness-representation); [physical connections](physical-connections.md#terms-and-capacity) |
| **Harness** | A fixed-width physical connector carrying a packed vector of 1–128 independently resolved bit nets in one logical channel. Its visible diameter does not grow with bit width. A one-bit harness is still the harness connector type. *Its combination/split tools may follow the first playable.* | [Harness representation](harnesses-and-net-links.md#harness-representation) |
| **Packed-vector bit order** | A harness or port uses indices `[width-1:0]`, with bit 0 least significant. In a new concatenation, the connector targeted first occupies the MSB side and the newly added connector the LSB side. | [Harness representation](harnesses-and-net-links.md#harness-representation); [universal bit order](harnesses-and-net-links.md#universal-bit-order) |
| **Net Link** | A visible, targetable, breakable wire-like stub with a semispherical cap. Stubs with matching case-sensitive link name, width, and scope connect corresponding bits without a continuous physical route; a Net Link still occupies a physical channel. | [Physical form](physical-connections.md#terms-and-capacity); [identity and scope](harnesses-and-net-links.md#net-link-identity-and-scope) |
| **Net Link scope** | The boundary within which a matching Net Link name and width creates electrical connectivity: top-level links have world scope; links packaged inside a module are private to each placed instance. | [Net Link identity and scope](harnesses-and-net-links.md#net-link-identity-and-scope) |
| **World-clock Net Link** | The special first-playable Net Link stub exposing the built-in world clock. Version 1 reserves `@world-clock` and marks it as a world-clock source; an ordinary named link cannot impersonate it. | [Clock controls](circuit-time-and-clock.md#clock-controls-and-unrendered-transitions); [saved connector record](first-playable-data-schema.md#connector-geometry-and-joins) |
| **Logical channel** | One of at most four independent connector paths a block space can contain. Channel occupancy is a physical placement constraint, separate from which electrical net a path belongs to. | [Capacity](physical-connections.md#terms-and-capacity); [authored topology](physical-connections.md#authored-topology-representation) |
| **Connector segment / route node / route span** | A segment is a targetable piece of a connector path. Version 1 records that path as nodes at exact positions and spans between node IDs; the visible mesh is derived from these records. | [Topology representation](physical-connections.md#authored-topology-representation); [route schema](first-playable-data-schema.md#connector-geometry-and-joins) |
| **Electrical net / bit net** | One electrically continuous signal derived from connector paths, explicit joins, module-port mappings, and valid matching Net Links. Each harness bit has a separate resolved bit net; the full harness is not one shorted signal. | [Terms and capacity](physical-connections.md#terms-and-capacity); [harness representation](harnesses-and-net-links.md#harness-representation); [Net Link scope](harnesses-and-net-links.md#net-link-identity-and-scope) |
| **Junction / join** | An explicit electrical connection made by targeting an existing connector when placing another, or by attaching a connector to a pin or port. A wire junction unites one-bit nets; equal-width harness junctions pair corresponding bits. The saved design records joins rather than inferring them from geometry. | [Junctions and crossings](physical-connections.md#junctions-and-crossings); [join records](first-playable-data-schema.md#connector-geometry-and-joins) |
| **Crossing** | Two visible connector paths that pass through the same cell without an explicit junction. They remain electrically separate even if their geometry appears near or over one another. | [Junctions and crossings](physical-connections.md#junctions-and-crossings); [visible topology](physical-connections.md#visible-topology) |
| **Combination point / concatenation** | *Later harness feature.* A distinct visible connection point that maps bits from selected connectors into a new wider harness, without shorting the input nets together or imposing signal direction. The existing targeted group occupies the MSB side. | [Concatenation](harnesses-and-net-links.md#direct-junction-versus-concatenation); [bit order](harnesses-and-net-links.md#universal-bit-order) |
| **Split point** | *Later harness feature.* A configurable connection point mapping selected harness bits or ranges to wires or other harnesses. It is not a separate block-sized splitter object. | [Split and conversion](harnesses-and-net-links.md#split-and-conversion) |
| **Width Adapter** | *Later component.* Explicitly converts connector width through chosen zero-extension, sign-extension, or truncation; an ordinary direct junction never performs a silent width conversion. | [Split and conversion](harnesses-and-net-links.md#split-and-conversion) |
| **Tag / ordinary tag** | A player-authored visible label on an object or connected ordinary wire net. Equal tags do **not** create electrical connectivity; Net Link names are a different field with electrical meaning. | [Player tagging](building-and-interface.md#player-presentation-and-tagging); [tag edits](physical-connections.md#crowded-selection-and-invalid-actions) |
| **Connector identity color** | A saved, player-selected visual identifier shown separately from the connector body's current signal-state color. It does not determine the electrical value. | [Connector appearance](physical-connections.md#connector-appearance) |
| **Signal-state color** | The derived color of a visible signal's current value: 0 muted blue, 1 bright green, X pulsing red, Z gray. It is distinct from a connector's saved identity color and from the single invalid-action red flash. | [Visuals](digital-simulation.md#visuals); [connector appearance](physical-connections.md#connector-appearance) |

## Modules and packaging

| Term | Meaning in SiliconSandbox | Full specification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Packaging / Package** | Copies a selected authored world circuit, including its exact internal 3D layout and electrical design, into a reusable fixed module-version definition. The original build stays in the world and has no live link to the new definition. | [Definition and instance](modules-and-packaging.md#definition-and-instance); [boundary extraction](modules-and-packaging.md#packaging-region-and-ports) |
| **Module** | A reusable circuit abstraction created by packaging, or the placed game object made from such a definition when context says “placed module.” Use the more precise terms **module version** and **module instance** when state or identity matters. | [Definition and instance](modules-and-packaging.md#definition-and-instance) |
| **Module family** | The durable identity grouping versions of the same reusable module; family membership alone does not make an incompatible interface safe for in-place upgrade. | [Creating versions](modules-and-packaging.md#creating-versions-and-upgrading); [identity model](modules-and-packaging.md#persistent-identity-model) |
| **Module version / fixed definition** | One immutable packaged snapshot of layout, topology, configuration, exterior ports, and exact nested child-version references. Existing placements remain pinned to the version they used; editing the original circuit does not change it. | [Canonical representation](modules-and-packaging.md#canonical-design-and-runtime-representation); [creating versions](modules-and-packaging.md#creating-versions-and-upgrading) |
| **Module instance / placed copy** | An individual placement of one exact module version. Instances may share read-only definition data, but each has its own internal signals, storage, events, and identity; placing two copies must not share mutable state. | [Definition and instance](modules-and-packaging.md#definition-and-instance); [captured versus live state](modules-and-packaging.md#what-is-captured) |
| **Nested module** | A module version placed inside another module's authored design. The parent records the exact child version; its live child state belongs to each parent-instance path. Recursive containment is invalid. | [Definition and instance](modules-and-packaging.md#definition-and-instance); [version records](first-playable-data-schema.md#module-versions-and-instances) |
| **Module port** | A named exterior input, output, or inout pin of a packaged module, with a width, bit order, physical face/quadrant, persistent identity, and mapping to exact internal bit connections. Ports let an instance connect to the outside world. | [Packaging region and ports](modules-and-packaging.md#packaging-region-and-ports); [port records](first-playable-data-schema.md#module-versions-and-instances) |
| **Port mapping** | The saved association between an exterior module-port bit and a specific internal connection endpoint or bit net. It is electrical mapping, not a screen position or a derived simulator-net index. | [Packaging region and ports](modules-and-packaging.md#packaging-region-and-ports); [port records](first-playable-data-schema.md#module-versions-and-instances) |
| **Interface snapshot** | The footprint and exterior-port information stored with a placed version-1 module instance so a missing-definition placeholder can preserve its pins and world connections without inventing the original internal circuitry. | [Recovery schema](first-playable-data-schema.md#global-library-and-recovery) |
| **Global module library** | Local durable collection of packaged fixed versions available across worlds. A saved world also embeds the exact versions it uses, so it does not depend on a newer library version replacing them. | [Library and world independence](modules-and-packaging.md#library-and-world-independence); [library schema](first-playable-data-schema.md#global-library-and-recovery) |
| **Missing-module placeholder** | A visibly marked stand-in when both the exact embedded and global definitions are missing or damaged. It retains known footprint, ports, and connections; output and inout ports drive X until an exact compatible definition is relinked. | [Library and world independence](modules-and-packaging.md#library-and-world-independence); [recovery schema](first-playable-data-schema.md#global-library-and-recovery) |
| **Live internal viewer** | Inspection view of a particular placed instance's saved internal 3D design with **that instance's current** internal values, including nested instances. Viewing does not edit the fixed definition. | [What is captured](modules-and-packaging.md#what-is-captured) |
| **Expand Module / physical re-expansion** | *Later feature.* Places a new editable world copy of a saved module's design and layout. It neither replaces a placed instance nor transfers that instance's transient state. | [Physical re-expansion](modules-and-packaging.md#physical-re-expansion-final-rule) |
| **Module version upgrade** | *Later feature.* A deliberate change of a placed instance to a compatible fixed version. Existing placements never update merely because a new version is packaged. | [Creating versions](modules-and-packaging.md#creating-versions-and-upgrading) |
| **Default module** | A supplied reusable module available through the game catalog, distinct from a player's newly packaged version. Freeplay exposes available defaults; Education introduces optimized supplied equivalents through progression. | [Modes](project-vision-and-world.md#modes-and-platform-scope); [education](education-and-onboarding.md) |

## Simulation and player actions

| Term | Meaning in SiliconSandbox | Full specification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Four-state signal** | Each simulated bit has value 0, 1, X (unknown), or Z (high impedance/no active driver). An undriven ordinary net resolves to Z; uninitialized ordinary stored state starts at X. | [Four-state behavior](digital-simulation.md#four-state-behavior) |
| **Derived simulation graph / netlist** | Rebuildable runtime connection and dependency data made from the authored world and exact module versions. It drives event simulation but is not a second authoritative saved design. | [Runtime architecture](digital-simulation.md#runtime-architecture-decision); [module representation](modules-and-packaging.md#canonical-design-and-runtime-representation) |
| **World clock** | The built-in default master clock signal, available through a specially identified link or connector. It does not gate combinational updates; stopping it is distinct from pausing all simulation. | [Clock and event model](circuit-time-and-clock.md#clock-and-event-model); [clock controls](circuit-time-and-clock.md#clock-controls-and-unrendered-transitions) |
| **Clock Source** | A separate player-placeable 0/1 square-wave source. Its independent frequency, phase controls, and relation to world-clock stop remain open for the later feature; it is not another name for the built-in world clock. | [Original catalog](components-and-rtl-timing.md#original-catalog-retained-alongside-clarified-components) |
| **Derived clock** | A clock signal created by player-built or provided circuitry, such as a divider, and fed to a component or module CLK pin. It is simulated from its own input transitions rather than replacing the built-in world clock. | [Clock and event model](circuit-time-and-clock.md#clock-and-event-model); [clock divider](components-and-rtl-timing.md#clock-divider) |
| **Constant Logic Source** | Configurable source of 1–128 four-state bits. When runtime On, it drives its configured value; when Off, it drives zero. Its saved initial On/Off state and value are distinct from a temporary right-click runtime toggle. | [Sources on load and reset](components-and-rtl-timing.md#sources-on-load-and-reset); [source persistence](saving-and-recovery.md#source-startup-state) |
| **Pulse Source** | *Later than the first playable.* Source whose click arms a pulse to begin at the next world-clock rising edge, for a configured whole number of cycles. Pending/active pulse behavior is part of the component rule; it is distinct from the world clock. | [Pulse source](components-and-rtl-timing.md#pulse-source); [milestone subset](delivery-and-acceptance.md#first-playable-professor-milestone) |
| **Reset Simulation** | Restarts ordinary simulation of the current authored design at simulated time zero and restores configured initial states, while keeping construction and specified persistent data. The world clock returns to stopped level 0 with its configured frequency. | [Reset rule](circuit-time-and-clock.md#clock-and-event-model); [saving boundary](saving-and-recovery.md#saved-design-versus-transient-simulation) |
| **Ordinary transient state / NVM exception** | Current signals, ordinary register and volatile-memory values, event queue, simulated time, and clock phase are not live checkpoints in a world save. *Later NVM feature:* runtime-writable nonvolatile contents are saved per instance and survive load/reset under their separate rule. | [Saved versus transient](saving-and-recovery.md#saved-design-versus-transient-simulation); [NVM exception](saving-and-recovery.md#nonvolatile-exception) |
| **Safe pause / safe edit boundary** | A pause after the current simulated time slot and all zero-time consequences settle. Structural edits and certain configuration changes use this boundary; a rejected edit makes no partial authored change. | [Safe pause and editing](circuit-time-and-clock.md#safe-pause-and-editing) |
| **Nonsettling feedback / convergence limit** | A zero-delay circuit that does not settle within the configured delta-pass guard. The game pauses safely, marks the affected signal/network X, and identifies the suspected area instead of processing forever. | [Nonsettling feedback](circuit-time-and-clock.md#nonsettling-feedback) |
| **Quick look** | The ordinary crosshair hover highlight/readout of a connector or pin's tag, width, and current value, without opening a detailed panel. | [Quick look and detailed inspection](inspection-and-diagnostics.md#quick-look-and-detailed-inspection) |
| **Inspect** | The detailed target-specific panel opened by the remappable I key, showing four-state values, bit indices, width, actual connections/drivers where known, and known fault causes. | [Quick look and detailed inspection](inspection-and-diagnostics.md#quick-look-and-detailed-inspection) |
| **Configure** | Targeted component-settings interface opened by the remappable C key; it changes authored parameters under the applicable safe-change and connection rules. It is distinct from operating a source's current runtime state. | [Inventory and component settings](building-and-interface.md#inventory-and-component-settings) |

## Distinctions to preserve

| Do not conflate | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Connector** and **electrical net** | A connector is authored physical geometry; a net is derived electrical continuity. Several connectors can form one net, and a harness contains multiple bit nets. |
| **Wire** and **harness** | A wire carries one bit; a harness is the packed-vector connector type, even if configured to width one. |
| **Ordinary tag** and **Net Link name** | A tag is a label; a matching Net Link name, width, and scope has electrical meaning. |
| **Module definition/version** and **module instance** | A version is immutable authored design; an instance is a placement with independent changing state. |
| **Authored design** and **derived graph/rendered geometry** | The authored design is durable source data; graph and graphics can be rebuilt from it. |
| **World clock** and **Clock Source** | One is the built-in clock; the other is a separate player-placeable component with later unresolved details. |
| **Configured initial source state** and **current runtime source state** | Load and Reset Simulation restore the configured initial state, not the transient result of a later click. |
| **Simulated circuit time** and **real-time throughput/FPS** | A circuit may represent a high-frequency clock without the game processing that many cycles each real second; rendering frames are a separate measure. |
