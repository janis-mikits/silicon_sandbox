## Clock and event model

The world clock is the default master clock signal for synchronous components, but it does not gate every simulation update. An event-driven engine updates affected combinational logic whenever its inputs change and updates edge-triggered components on their configured clock edge. Player-built and provided clock dividers can create derived clocks; a wire, harness, or Net Link can feed a component or module CLK pin with a valid signal. World-clock frequency is configurable; its initial level is 0, and its confirmed default duty cycle is 50 percent. Make the world clock available through a clearly identified link or connector.

Simulation time, world-clock transitions, playback speed, and rendered frames are distinct. One clock edge may cause sequential evaluation, nonblocking updates, combinational propagation, and new derived-clock edges within the same simulated time slot. Preserve the needed event ordering and delta-cycle behavior. Slow playback changes how quickly simulated time advances for the player; it must not change the circuit’s simulated relationships. If the engine cannot keep up, report the achieved speed rather than silently skipping edges. Manual controls can advance one world-clock edge or one complete world-clock cycle, settling all consequences before returning control. A circuit continues to simulate when it is out of view, even if its geometry is not rendered.

**Clock-scale decision, 30 September 2026:** The professor demonstration uses a 10 Hz world clock. For the first playable, a new world's default world-clock frequency is **10 Hz**, and the configurable frequency range is **0.1 through 100 Hz, inclusive**; stopping the clock remains a separate control. This first-playable range does not cap later clocks. Simulation-time precision is **1 picosecond**. The scheduler must distinguish the transitions of a later simulated 1 GHz, 50-percent-duty-cycle clock (a 1 ns period and 0.5 ns half-period) while preserving the event-ordering and delta-settling rules above. For configured frequencies whose edge times are not exact multiples of 1 picosecond, scheduling must avoid accumulating unbounded rounding drift. Long-running simulation must not silently wrap or corrupt its timestamp; the representation below settles this foundation. The later player-facing frequency range and high-frequency throughput target remain open. A simulated 1 GHz clock is not a promise to process one billion cycles per real second; the first playable has no 1 GHz performance acceptance test.

**World start and reopen decisions, 30 September 2026:** A newly created first-playable world starts with its world clock stopped, at the specified initial logic level 0. Reopening a saved world also starts the world clock stopped at level 0, retaining its saved configured frequency rather than restoring a transient running state. Pressing P starts it. Stopping the world clock does not pause combinational simulation or prevent live source changes from propagating.

**Stop/resume phase decision, 5 October 2026:** Stopping the world clock freezes its current level and the remaining simulated time to its next edge. Resuming at a later simulation time waits that remainder before the next edge, then continues alternating at the configured frequency. A frequency change while the clock is stopped discards the frozen remainder and begins a fresh half-period at the new frequency when resumed. A manual edge step consumes exactly the next edge, settles its consequences, and leaves the clock stopped with the next interval ready; a cycle step consumes two such edges. This does not change the rule that a frequency change requested while running first completes the current interval at the old frequency.

**Reset Simulation decision, 30 September 2026:** Reset Simulation returns simulated time to zero, restores ordinary transient component state under each component's defined initialization/reset rules, and returns the world clock to logic level 0 with its phase restarted and its configured frequency retained. The clock remains stopped until the player presses P. Construction and the specified persistent data are unaffected under the [save/reset rules](saving-and-recovery.md#saved-design-versus-transient-simulation).

**Initial CLK observation decision, 5 October 2026:** On new world, reopen, or Reset Simulation, first settle configured sources and the initial world-clock level, then establish each edge-triggered component's previous-CLK observation from its settled CLK net without treating that starting value as a transition. Do the same for a newly placed edge-triggered component at a safe structural-edit boundary; existing components retain their prior CLK observations. An initially undriven CLK may therefore be Z, or a newly attached CLK may already be high, without inventing a sampling event or erasing authored initial storage. Subsequent live CLK transitions use the four-state positive-edge table below.

## First-playable scheduler contract

**Decision, 30 September 2026:** Represent nonnegative simulated time as a checked pair `(wholeSeconds, picosecondsWithinSecond)`, with an unsigned 64-bit whole-second count and an integer fractional count from 0 through 999,999,999,999. Compare times lexicographically, normalize every addition, and detect overflow before scheduling or advancing; on exhaustion, pause safely with a clear limit diagnostic instead of wrapping. This has 1-picosecond resolution and can distinguish a later 1 GHz clock's 500-picosecond half-period without requiring that rate in real time. A scheduled event also has a delta-cycle/phase order and stable insertion order; these are runtime data, not save data.

Parse the saved decimal world-clock frequency as an exact rational request. Schedule the alternating 50-percent-duty edges using a remainder/phase accumulator or equivalent computation from the original rational half-period, rounding absolute edge times to the nearest picosecond. An exact half-picosecond tie rounds to the nearest even picosecond (decision, 5 October 2026), making the rare tie deterministic without a systematic upward bias. Do not repeatedly add an already rounded half-period, which would accumulate drift. Reject a configured frequency if distinct required edges would collapse onto one timestamp at this precision. The first-playable 0.1–100 Hz range and later 1 GHz example do not have that problem. A frequency change completes the current high/low interval under the old setting, then starts the new rational schedule for future intervals under the existing [safe-change rule](#safe-pause-and-editing).

For the supported first-playable subset, process each timestamp to a settled state before advancing time or completing a manual step. Apply queued player source changes and scheduled world-clock transitions in deterministic insertion order at the start of the slot. If a player source change and a world-clock edge land at the same timestamp, the source change takes effect before edge-triggered sampling. Resolve affected nets and combinational logic until quiescent, then detect the specified CLK transitions and sample edge-triggered components from the settled pre-update values. Commit their scheduled state updates together, propagate their output changes, and repeat delta processing for any newly produced edges until settled. A component does not see another component's uncommitted edge update in the same sampling phase. A live source action received during a slot is queued for the next safe boundary; it does not alter a slot already being settled. This defines game input ordering for the component subset, not a blanket claim of full SystemVerilog scheduling compatibility.

The configurable convergence guard defaults to 1,024 repeated feedback delta passes for one timestamp. Ordinary acyclic propagation is processed as worklist activity and must not be mislabeled an oscillator merely because a long path has many gates. Hitting the guard invokes the existing [nonsettling procedure](#nonsettling-feedback); tests must distinguish stable storage, a converging feedback network, and a zero-delay oscillator. Exact queue data structures and worklist optimizations remain implementation choices.

## Four-state positive-edge detection

**Decision, 30 September 2026:** Every rising-edge-triggered component uses the same CLK transition rule from [IEEE 1800-2023 §9.4.2/Table 9-2](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10458102): 0→1, 0→X, 0→Z, X→1, and Z→1 count as positive edges. Other transitions do not count as positive edges. A component samples its inputs and applies its own specified next-state rule on a detected edge after the relevant event-ordering steps; its response to X/Z on data, reset, enable, or other controls remains component-specific. An uncertain transition on CLK that appears in the positive-edge list is a sampling event, not an instruction to merge an edge result with a no-edge result. This rule applies to the world clock, derived clocks, and player-connected CLK nets when their values make those transitions. It does not by itself settle negative-edge-triggered behavior or every component's unknown-control truth table.

## Safe pause and editing

Pressing Escape requests a pause. Finish all events at the current simulation time, including zero-time settling, then stop before the next time slot. Preserve the event queue, clock phase, simulation time, and component state so Resume continues exactly. A temporary “Pausing” indication is acceptable. Structural edits such as placing, breaking, reconnecting, rotating, changing a bit mapping, or changing a pin configuration auto-pause at a safe boundary. Operating a live switch or logic source remains possible while running. The confirmed live-change rule is that a world-clock frequency change affects only future behavior and never rewrites elapsed simulation history. The request is applied after the current simulation time slot and all zero-time settling finish. The clock keeps its current logic level and completes the current high or low interval at the old frequency; the next interval and all later intervals use the new frequency. If the world clock is stopped, the new setting is saved and used when the clock resumes. Frequency must be positive: zero and negative values are invalid, and stopping the clock remains a separate control.

**First-playable paused source action decision, 5 October 2026:** At a settled simulation-pause boundary, a player may toggle a Constant Logic Source. Its combinational effects settle at the current simulation timestamp without advancing the world clock or time. If simulation is running, the action enters the current safe event slot and settles before the next clock edge. Neither action edits the source's saved startup configuration.

**All-or-nothing structural edit and packaging decision, 30 September 2026:** At the safe boundary, prepare and validate each complete placement, break, reconnection, rotation, group edit, and packaging operation before publishing its authored change. Validate the affected occupancy, topology, pins, ports, footprint, and references under their existing rules. A successful operation publishes one coherent authored revision and brings the derived simulation graph, renderer and selection data, and edit history into agreement with it before simulation or interaction resumes. A rejected operation makes no partial change to the authored design or to the simulation state reached at the safe boundary; the safe pause itself may remain, and the existing invalid-action feedback applies. Packaging creates the fixed definition and its corresponding library/inventory reference together, or creates neither, while leaving the source circuit in place. Save snapshots and visible selection results must not reflect a partially published revision. This rule does not choose the graph-update algorithm or change existing damaged-save recovery rules.

## Nonsettling feedback

The delta-cycle convergence limit is configurable. Stable storage and a legal latch are distinct from a zero-delay oscillator. The accepted process is:

> If the convergence limit is exceeded, SiliconSandbox shall stop processing the unstable circuit, pause simulation safely, mark the affected signal or network as X, explain that a zero-delay oscillation or non-converging feedback loop was detected, and preserve the world so the player can correct the circuit.

The feedback points to the affected area. The 1-picosecond simulation-time precision, checked timestamp representation, and first-playable 0.1–100 Hz world-clock range are fixed above; the later supported frequency range and phase behavior for newly introduced independent clocks remain open in [Appendix B](open-decisions-and-cautions.md).

## Clock controls and unrendered transitions

The world-clock frequency is set through the Escape settings menu; P starts or stops the world clock. Stopping this signal is distinct from pausing all simulation: combinational logic still responds to live sources when the world clock is stopped. The simulator processes every electrical transition, including transitions too fast to animate frame by frame. The core game retains the current settled value and the limited diagnostic information needed to explain errors; it does not keep a complete transition-history log. The clock link is a specially identified Net Link under the same physical-channel rules as other Net Links.
