# 11 Education and onboarding

Education is the lowest-priority major mode. The long-term aspiration includes hundreds of hours of material associated with Purdue ECE 270, ECE 337, and ECE 437, but the course map, lesson sequence, challenge library, hints, and exact unlock list were deliberately left for later. Freeplay, the simulator, building, packaging, and reliable saving come first. Freeplay exposes its default components and available modules without an education unlock ladder. Freeplay also exposes the available educational material immediately, as specified in the original design. Education starts with one-bit primitives and no ready-made multi-bit logic gates or modules; learners build their own and later unlock optimized configurable-width versions. This progression teaches abstraction rather than treating an optimized block as magic.

For a future lesson, record objective, prerequisites, allowed components, test inputs and sequences, expected outputs, completion criteria, and unlock. Judge observable behavior and allow different correct designs within the allowed parts; a particular construction method may be required when it is the lesson objective. Passing a finite test set means passing those tests, not formal proof of every input sequence. Let players inspect failures, revise, retry, and revisit lessons. Hints and fuller explanations can be added later. Educational unlocks belong to a local player profile across worlds, with each education world retaining its current lesson position.

The eventual regular game gets a short introduction covering movement, connector/component placement, signal inspection, operation of the world clock, and packaging. The first playable professor demonstration does not require this introduction because the user will demonstrate and explain that build personally. The first public release does include it. The division between player-built memory/register systems, default modules, and later advanced integrated systems still needs curriculum design.
